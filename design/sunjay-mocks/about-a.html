Para Theater, with the Elkdonis Arts Collective

Jason Ford is an artist, a rock balancer and a practitioner. He teaches at Para Theater, a paratheatre medium for expressing the internal landscape, and he keeps its archive: teachings, practices and creative explorations, set down in the order they were made rather than the order they were meant to be read.

Rock balancing is the plainest thing he does and the best explanation of the rest. A stone is set down, felt for, and either holds or does not. Nothing is argued with. The attention it asks for is the same attention he asks of people in the labs, and it is why this page is built as a stack that grows while you read.

What follows is the long version. It is written to be read slowly, with the margins open.

Work on self comes first

The labs begin where the collective begins: with work on self, and only then, slowly, work on the world. The collective describes its method as self exploratory, reductionist ritual and theatre. Reductionist here means taking things away until what is left can be looked at.

You don’t force attention, you invoke it. Attention comes from space around you, not your ego.

From “Wearing a cloak of Garuda feathers”

People often arrive expecting a stage. There is none. A lab is a space for solitary work on self, done alongside others, in what the labs call an asocial context. The word is chosen with care.

Asocial is not antisocial, it indicates neutrality.

From “Q: What is Ritual Theatre?”

Nobody performs for anybody. Nobody is asked to be interesting. The work is private and the room is shared, and that is the whole arrangement.

Sitting is not arrival

He is as wary of meditation as a destination as he is of theatre as a spectacle. Sitting is a way of keeping the instrument in tune so that the real work can be done. In the words of his own essay, it is tempering the blade.

Sitting is not arrival. It is tempering the blade, tuning the instrument, so the real Work can be done.

From “Why we meditate”

The same suspicion runs through the writing. Practices are offered as tools to pick up and put down, and the essays on this site read less like doctrine than like field notes kept by someone still in the middle of it.

Where it comes from

The collective names its lineage plainly. Its circle of seers works in the tradition of Antero Alli, Jerzy Grotowski, G.I. Gurdjieff, E.J. Gold and Carlos Castaneda. Those names are listed below as the collective lists them; this page claims nothing more about who taught whom.

The collective itself began in Newmarket, Ontario, in 1990, and now works between Paris, Los Angeles and Toronto. Para Theater is its online room.

What the labs ask of you

The invitation is to sincere friends willing to explore this self initiatory ritual technology in online sessions over Zoom. It is not a drop-in class and it is not a lecture series.

If you can make regular meetings and uphold your commitments, you are welcome to join the circle. Regularity matters more than talent, and showing up matters more than understanding.

The collective is not commercial. Its works are intended to be experienced, not sold, and each inquiry rests on one question grounded in what it is to be human.

Sessions are held online, so the circle is not limited by place. The first meeting is a conversation, and neither side commits to anything beyond it.

A practice, in order

Years are left open where they are not yet confirmed. The order is the point.
