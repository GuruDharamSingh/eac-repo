Surface UI, as elktours draws it

One object at three sizes. A face is the card in a grid, a surface is the popup it opens (all surfaces stack inside one dialog), and a page is the same masthead, body and foot in the page flow. Everything below is drawn from the source and the elktours token map; nothing has been redesigned yet.

An app themes the surface by setting --eac-surface-* and --eac-kind-* in its own :root. Each kind gets an accent that colours the face's edge, the medallion, the masthead hairline and the primary button.

02

Faces

The whole card is one hit target, drawn behind inert content, so a face can carry live controls (a month grid, an input, RSVP pills) that take their own clicks. A click elsewhere opens the surface; with only href it navigates and the cue becomes →.

The surface

Every popup composes the same frame: masthead, body with an optional rail, and a foot with one primary action in the kind's colour. A surface never lays itself out, which is why popups built weeks apart in different apps match.

08

What this means for the two new pieces

  • A place card can be a face. Medallion, kicker, title, blurb and a preview slot already exist; the preview could carry the map tile, and SurfaceFacts covers address, hours and link.
  • Places have no kind. They borrow "neutral", so they get the muted accent and the • glyph. A "place" kind (glyph and accent) would set map and place cards apart from tours.
  • Nothing draws a map yet. Faces, popups and catalogue rows all show position as text. pin-map.tsx and places-map.tsx exist on the manage side and could be wrapped as a preview.
  • The expandable row is native <details> with Tailwind. A mini card form for a list (route stops, say) could reuse the summary row shape and put surface fields and facts in the open body.
  • Two label voices. Surface uses mono uppercase kickers; the business card and catalogue use opacity and muted text. Picking one is part of merging.
