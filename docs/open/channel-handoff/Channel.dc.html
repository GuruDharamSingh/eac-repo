On painting the marsh
Three mornings at low tide, and what the water leaves when it goes.

The marsh only holds still for about an hour. At low tide the channels empty to a skin of silver, and the grass stands up out of the mud in clumps that look, from the bank, like brushwork already done.

I set the easel on the boardwalk the first morning and lost the light before I had the horizon in. The second morning I came earlier and painted only the water: the pale band where the sky sits in it, and the darker cuts where it runs off toward the river.

By the third morning the painting was mostly a record of the first two. I left the passages that went wrong
